# **Frequently Asked Questions**

Have questions about working together? Here are answers to some of the things people often wonder about when they’re considering therapy.

---

## What can I expect during my first appointment?

Many people start with a free 15-minute consultation to see if working together feels like a good fit. If it does, we’ll schedule a 60-minute intake session.

In that first meeting, I’ll ask some questions to understand what’s bringing you in and what you’re hoping for. We’ll go at a pace that feels right for you—you don’t have to share everything at once.

We’ll also pay attention to how the fit feels. If it’s not the right match, I’m happy to help you find someone who is.

---

## How do I know if therapy is right for me?

People come to therapy for many different reasons—sometimes because they’re going through a difficult period, and sometimes because they want support in understanding themselves or their relationships more deeply.

You don’t have to be in crisis to benefit from therapy. If something in your life feels stuck, overwhelming, or simply worth exploring, therapy can be a place to think about it with support.

If you’re unsure whether therapy is the right next step, a consultation call can be a helpful way to talk it through.

---

## How long will I need to be in therapy?

The length of therapy depends on your goals and the pace that feels right for you.

Some people come to therapy for help with a specific issue and find that a few months of focused work is enough. Others choose to stay longer as they continue exploring patterns, relationships, and personal growth over time.

We’ll check in along the way so that our work stays aligned with what you want.

## Is individual therapy a prerequisite for couples therapy? I feel like I have to do my “own” work first. 

Many people assume they need to do their “own work” before starting couples therapy. In this approach, that’s not required.

In my approach to couples work (Relational Life Therapy), individual work is done within the context of the relationship whenever possible. The patterns that create distress don’t live in isolation—they show up between you.

Doing this work together helps each partner better understand the other’s inner experience as it unfolds. This builds empathy, supports individual growth, and strengthens the relationship—rather than treating these as separate processes.

## Do you offer in-person or online sessions?

I offer virtual sessions only.

Meeting online allows many people to fit therapy more easily into their lives without the stress of commuting or finding parking. Research shows that online therapy can be just as effective as in-person therapy, so you won’t be sacrificing quality or effectiveness for flexibility and convenience.

All sessions take place through a secure, HIPAA-compliant video platform designed to protect your privacy. 

Getting started is simple. Before your first appointment, you’ll complete intake paperwork through a secure client portal. Once I receive it, I’ll confirm your appointment and send a link for joining the session.

## Do you accept insurance?

I am not in-network with insurance plans. However, I am considered an out-of-network provider.

This means you pay for sessions at the time of service. If your insurance plan includes out-of-network benefits (which many PPO plans do), you may be able to receive partial reimbursement by submitting documentation. I am happy to provide a monthly superbill for this purpose.

You can also contact your insurance provider directly to ask about your out-of-network mental health benefits.
