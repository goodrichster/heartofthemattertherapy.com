Option G
Approach Services Best Fit
Cutting Edge Contrast

Unexpected color. Sharper thinking.

This concept is intentionally more provocative than the other directions. It keeps the practice credible and high-end, but uses deeper contrast, a less expected palette, and a more asymmetrical layout to spark fresh reactions and break out of generic therapy-site patterns.

See the rationale

Design aim

Push the visual language without losing trust. The site should still feel grounded, articulate, and premium, but with more tension, energy, and memorability.

Why it matters

If every option stays too safe, it becomes harder to see what a more distinctive brand could do for the practice.

Palette Logic

Petrol blue and deep teal establish authority. Burnt coral brings warmth and edge. Cream keeps the overall experience human and accessible rather than cold.

Still upscale. Just less expected.

This layout uses offset sections and stronger color-blocking instead of the usual soft neutrals and centered hero patterns. The result feels editorial and design-led, but not trendy for its own sake.

It could work especially well for a therapist who is direct, perceptive, and wants the brand to feel sophisticated rather than merely soothing.

How the content would behave in this system.

Homepage

Large, declarative headline. Minimal intro text. More visual contrast and stronger section breaks.

About Page

Portrait, credentials, and therapist voice would be given more dramatic pacing and negative space.

Services

Individual and couples work can be separated with more typographic presence and fewer standard cards.

Best when the goal is differentiation.

This is the right outlier option if you want to pressure-test whether the brand could support a more art-directed identity. Even if it is not the final choice, it can reveal how much boldness actually feels right.

Main caution

The copy and photography would need to stay disciplined. Without that restraint, a stronger palette can overpower the therapeutic message.

Option G is designed as a creative provocation: still aligned with the original goals, but bold enough to open new conversations about how distinctive this practice could look.
